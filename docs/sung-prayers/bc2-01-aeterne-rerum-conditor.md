| Attribution | **Written by** Ambrose. One of the four hymns accepted as his by all scholars; St. Augustine names it as Ambrose's (*Retractationes* I.21) and quotes it. |

| Theme | Night ending, the cock crowing: Peter's tears at cockcrow, the fallen rising, Christ as the first light. |

## The text

Latin as printed by Walpole, with capitals for the screen. English ours.

**1**
> Aeterne rerum conditor,
> noctem diemque qui regis,
> et temporum das tempora,
> ut alleues fastidium;

*Eternal Maker of all things, / who rule the night and day, / and give the seasons each their time / to lighten our weariness:*

**2**
> praeco diei iam sonat,
> noctis profundae peruigil,
> nocturna lux uiantibus,
> a nocte noctem segregans.

*the herald of the day now sounds, / wakeful through the deep night, / a light by night for travellers, / marking off the watches of the night.*

**3**
> Hoc excitatus lucifer
> soluit polum caligine,
> hoc omnis errorum chorus
> uias nocendi deserit.

*At his call the morning star awakes / and frees the sky from gloom; / at his call the whole roving throng / forsakes its ways of harm.*

**4**
> Hoc nauta uires colligit
> pontique mitescunt freta;
> hoc ipse petra ecclesiae
> canente culpam diluit.

*At his call the sailor takes heart / and the swells of the sea grow calm; / at his crowing the Rock of the Church himself / washed away his fault.*

**5**
> Surgamus ergo strenue:
> gallus iacentes excitat
> et somnolentos increpat;
> gallus negantes arguit.

*Let us rise, then, and be quick: / the cock wakes those who lie abed / and scolds the drowsy; / the cock rebukes all who deny.*

**6**
> Gallo canente spes redit,
> aegris salus refunditur,
> mucro latronis conditur,
> lapsis fides reuertitur.

*When the cock crows, hope returns, / health flows back into the sick, / the robber's blade is sheathed, / faith comes back to the fallen.*

**7**
> Iesu, labantes respice,
> et nos uidendo corrige;
> si respicis, lapsus cadunt,
> fletuque culpa soluitur.

*Jesus, look on us who waver, / and set us right by your gaze; / if you look on us, our failings fall, / and guilt is dissolved in tears.*

**8**
> Tu lux refulge sensibus
> mentisque somnum discute;
> te nostra uox primum sonet
> et uota soluamus tibi.

*Shine, O Light, upon our senses, / shake the sleep from our minds; / let our voice sound first for you, / and let us pay our vows to you.*

Notes for the description: "the herald" is the cock; stanza 4 is Peter, who wept when the cock crowed after his denial (Lk 22:61–62); stanza 7's "look" is Christ turning to look at Peter.

## Style

```
contemplative gregorian jazz, solo male chant vocal in latin, plainchant phrasing, free rhythm, dorian mode opening into mixolydian light, low drone, upright bass, soft rhodes, flugelhorn answering the voice, distant church bell, stone church reverb, night turning to dawn, hope, awakening, slow building to quiet radiance, no drum groove, no pop chorus
```

## Lyrics (for Suno or Gemini)

Sung spelling uses *v* (alleves, pervigil…) so the engine doesn't sing "oo"; the screen keeps Walpole's *u*. Same words. All 8 stanzas fit about 3 minutes at a flowing pace; for Gemini's 3-minute cap, ask for "about 3 minutes" and check that stanza 8 made it in.

```
[Intro: low drone, distant bell, before dawn]

[Verse 1: solo voice, almost unaccompanied]
Aeterne rerum conditor,
noctem diemque qui regis,
et temporum das tempora,
ut alleves fastidium;

[Verse 2]
praeco diei iam sonat,
noctis profundae pervigil,
nocturna lux viantibus,
a nocte noctem segregans.

[Verse 3: bass enters]
Hoc excitatus lucifer
solvit polum caligine,
hoc omnis errorum chorus
vias nocendi deserit.

[Verse 4: hushed]
Hoc nauta vires colligit
pontique mitescunt freta;
hoc ipse petra ecclesiae
canente culpam diluit.

[Instrumental: flugelhorn answers]

[Verse 5: rising]
Surgamus ergo strenue:
gallus iacentes excitat
et somnolentos increpat;
gallus negantes arguit.

[Verse 6]
Gallo canente spes redit,
aegris salus refunditur,
mucro latronis conditur,
lapsis fides revertitur.

[Verse 7: tender, slower]
Iesu, labantes respice,
et nos videndo corrige;
si respicis, lapsus cadunt,
fletuque culpa solvitur.

[Verse 8: fullest, dawn light]
Tu lux refulge sensibus
mentisque somnum discute;
te nostra vox primum sonet
et vota solvamus tibi.

[End: drone fades, one bell]
```
