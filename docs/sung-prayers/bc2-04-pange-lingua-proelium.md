# Blue Chant II · 04 · Pange lingua gloriosi proelium certaminis — St. Venantius Fortunatus

Track 4 of [Blue Chant, Volume II](chants.md): the song of the battle of the Cross. New melody (Suno). Not committed.

Audio: `work/sung/bc2/04/audio.wav`, 5:32 (Nov 18). Whisper hears all 10 stanzas in order, nothing added: v1 at 0:15, instrumental 2:35–3:05, v8 *Crux fidelis* at 3:57, last line ends ~5:19. Listen: opening word *Pange* (Whisper: "Angeli") `check-a`; stanza 7 *sanguis, unda profluit* `check-b`; stanza 10 and ending `check-c`.

**Not Aquinas's *Pange lingua*.** Aquinas's *Pange lingua gloriosi corporis mysterium* (sung prayer 06) borrows this hymn's first line. Fortunatus's hymn came seven centuries earlier: 569 against c. 1264. Keep the full title, *Pange lingua gloriosi proelium certaminis*, on screen and in files, so the two are never confused.

## The text

Latin as printed by Walpole, with capitals for the screen. English ours.

**1**
> Pange, lingua, gloriosi proelium certaminis,
> et super crucis tropaeo dic triumphum nobilem,
> qualiter redemptor orbis inmolatus uicerit.

*Sing, my tongue, the battle of the glorious struggle, / and over the trophy of the Cross proclaim the noble triumph: / how the world's Redeemer, sacrificed, won the victory.*

**2**
> De parentis protoplasti fraude factor condolens,
> quando pomi noxialis morte morsu conruit,
> ipse lignum tunc notauit, damna ligni ut solueret.

*The Maker, grieving at the deceit done to our first father, / when, biting the deadly fruit, he fell into death, / himself marked out a tree even then, to undo the harm of the tree.*

**3**
> Hoc opus nostrae salutis ordo depoposcerat,
> multiformis perditoris arte ut artem falleret,
> et medellam ferret inde, hostis unde laeserat.

*This work of our salvation God's plan had required: / that craft should outwit the craft of the many-shaped destroyer, / and bring the cure from where the enemy had dealt the wound.*

**4**
> Quando uenit ergo sacri plenitudo temporis,
> missus est ab arce patris natus, orbis conditor,
> atque uentre uirginali carne factus prodiit.

*So when the fullness of the sacred time had come, / the Son, the world's Maker, was sent from the Father's high throne, / and came forth from a virgin's womb, made flesh.*

**5**
> Uagit infans inter arta conditus praesepia;
> membra pannis inuoluta uirgo mater adligat,
> et pedes manusque, crura stricta pingit fascia.

*The infant cries, hidden in the narrow manger; / his virgin mother wraps his limbs in rags and binds them, / hands and feet; the tight band marks his legs.*

**6**
> Lustra sex qui iam peracta, tempus inplens corporis,
> se uolente, natus ad hoc, passioni deditus,
> agnus in crucis leuatur inmolandus stipite.

*When thirty years were now complete, his time in the flesh fulfilled, / of his own will, born for this, given up to the Passion, / the Lamb is lifted onto the trunk of the Cross to be sacrificed.*

**7**
> Hic acetum, fel, harundo, sputa, claui, lancea;
> mite corpus perforatur; sanguis, unda profluit,
> terra, pontus, astra, mundus quo lauantur flumine.

*Here are vinegar, gall, the reed, spittle, nails, the lance; / the gentle body is pierced; blood and water flow out, / a stream that washes earth, sea, stars, the whole world.*

**8**
> Crux fidelis, inter omnes arbor una nobilis,
> nulla talem silua profert flore, fronde, germine;
> dulce lignum, dulce clauo dulce pondus sustinens.

*Faithful Cross, the one noble tree among all trees: / no forest brings forth your like in flower, leaf, or bud. / Sweet the wood, sweet the nails, holding so sweet a weight.*

**9**
> Flecte ramos, arbor alta, tensa laxa uiscera,
> et rigor lentescat ille, quem dedit natiuitas,
> ut superni membra regis mite tendas stipite.

*Bend your branches, lofty tree, relax your taut fibres, / and let that hardness soften that nature gave you, / so that you hold the limbs of the King on high on a gentle trunk.*

**10**
> Sola digna tu fuisti ferre pretium saeculi,
> atque portum praeparare nauta mundo naufrago,
> quem sacer cruor perunxit fusus agni corpore.

*You alone were worthy to bear the ransom of the world, / and, like a sailor, to make ready a harbour for a shipwrecked world, / you, anointed by the holy blood poured from the body of the Lamb.*

Notes for the description:
- Stanza 2 alludes to the old legend that the wood of the Cross came from the tree of Eden. Label it as legend: "tradition held…".
- The *Crux fidelis* stanza (8) is the one the Church sings at the Veneration of the Cross on Good Friday.
- Stanza 10's "sailor" is the Cross as an ark for a shipwrecked world. Walpole notes Ambrose's image of the Cross as the ship of salvation.
- Both of the album's Fortunatus hymns (03, 04) were written for the same day in 569.

Walpole OCR fixes, to confirm on the page images: *dic* (OCR "die"), *quem* (OCR "quern"), *corpore* (OCR "corpora").

## Style

```
contemplative gregorian jazz, solo male chant vocal in latin, marching trochaic pulse felt not played, plainchant phrasing, dorian mode, low drone, upright bass walking slowly, soft rhodes, muted trumpet answering the voice, distant bell, stone church reverb, solemn and triumphant, the cross as victory, slow building to quiet glory, no drum groove, no pop chorus
```

## Lyrics (for Suno or Gemini)

Sung spelling uses *v* (vicerit, uentre → ventre…) and *inm-/inp-* → *imm-/imp-*; the screen keeps Walpole's *u* and spelling. Same words. With ten long stanzas, this runs about 5–6 minutes, which is fine for Suno. For Gemini's ~3-minute cap, make it in two parts: 1–5 and 6–10.

```
[Intro: low drone, distant bell]

[Verse 1: solo voice, almost unaccompanied]
Pange, lingua, gloriosi proelium certaminis,
et super crucis tropaeo dic triumphum nobilem,
qualiter redemptor orbis immolatus vicerit.

[Verse 2]
De parentis protoplasti fraude factor condolens,
quando pomi noxialis morte morsu corruit,
ipse lignum tunc notavit, damna ligni ut solveret.

[Verse 3: bass enters]
Hoc opus nostrae salutis ordo depoposcerat,
multiformis perditoris arte ut artem falleret,
et medellam ferret inde, hostis unde laeserat.

[Verse 4]
Quando venit ergo sacri plenitudo temporis,
missus est ab arce patris natus, orbis conditor,
atque ventre virginali carne factus prodiit.

[Verse 5: tender, hushed]
Vagit infans inter arta conditus praesepia;
membra pannis involuta virgo mater alligat,
et pedes manusque, crura stricta pingit fascia.

[Instrumental: muted trumpet answers]

[Verse 6: darker]
Lustra sex qui iam peracta, tempus implens corporis,
se volente, natus ad hoc, passioni deditus,
agnus in crucis levatur immolandus stipite.

[Verse 7]
Hic acetum, fel, harundo, sputa, clavi, lancea;
mite corpus perforatur; sanguis, unda profluit,
terra, pontus, astra, mundus quo lavantur flumine.

[Verse 8: fullest]
Crux fidelis, inter omnes arbor una nobilis,
nulla talem silva profert flore, fronde, germine;
dulce lignum, dulce clavo dulce pondus sustinens.

[Verse 9: softer, pleading]
Flecte ramos, arbor alta, tensa laxa viscera,
et rigor lentescat ille, quem dedit nativitas,
ut superni membra regis mite tendas stipite.

[Verse 10: quiet triumph]
Sola digna tu fuisti ferre pretium saeculi,
atque portum praeparare nauta mundo naufrago,
quem sacer cruor perunxit fusus agni corpore.

[End: drone fades, one bell]
```
No repeats, no added words, no doxology (not Fortunatus's).
