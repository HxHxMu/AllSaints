## Where the sung (1632) text differs from Ambrose

| Line | Sung (Roman 1632) | Ambrose (Walpole) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | *diem dies illuminans* | *dies dierum inluminans* |
| 10–11 | *Patrem potentis gratiae, / Patrem perennis gloriae* | the two lines in the other order |
| 12 | *culpam relegat* | *culpam releget* |
| 13–16 | *Confirmet actus strenuos… agenda recte dirigat* | *informet actus strenuos, dentem retundat inuidi, casus secundet asperos, donet gerendi gratiam* |
| 18 | *sit pura nobis castitas* | *casto fideli corpore* |
| 24 | *profusionem Spiritus* | *ebrietatem Spiritus* ("the sober drunkenness of the Spirit": Ambrose's own famous phrase, softened in 1632) |
| 29–30 | *Aurora lucem provehit, cum luce nobis prodeat* | *aurora cursus prouehit, aurora totus prodeat* |
| 33–36 | doxology | none |

So the credit says "St. Ambrose; Roman Breviary text (1632)", never just "St. Ambrose" as if every word were his. The description can mention *ebrietas Spiritus*: it's a lovely detail.

## The text (as sung)

Latin as printed in the 1912 *Antiphonale* (capitals and punctuation for the screen). English ours.

**1**
> Splendor paternae gloriae,
> de luce lucem proferens,
> lux lucis et fons luminis,
> diem dies illuminans:

*Radiance of the Father's glory, / bringing forth light from light, / Light of light and fount of light, / Day that makes the day shine:*

**2**
> Verusque sol, illabere,
> micans nitore perpeti:
> iubarque Sancti Spiritus
> infunde nostris sensibus.

*true Sun, glide down upon us, / gleaming with unending brightness, / and pour the Holy Spirit's radiance / into all our senses.*

**3**
> Votis vocemus et Patrem,
> Patrem potentis gratiae,
> Patrem perennis gloriae:
> culpam relegat lubricam.

*In prayer let us call on the Father too, / Father of mighty grace, / Father of everlasting glory: / may he banish the guilt that makes us slip.*

**4**
> Confirmet actus strenuos:
> dentes retundat invidi:
> casus secundet asperos:
> agenda recte dirigat.

*May he make our deeds strong and steady, / blunt the teeth of the envious one, / turn hard chances to good, / and guide aright what we must do.*

**5**
> Mentem gubernet et regat:
> sit pura nobis castitas:
> fides calore ferveat,
> fraudis venena nesciat.

*May he steer and rule our mind; / may our purity be clean; / may faith burn with warmth / and know nothing of deceit's poison.*

**6**
> Christusque nobis sit cibus,
> potusque noster sit fides:
> laeti bibamus sobriam
> profusionem Spiritus.

*May Christ be our food, / and faith our drink; / let us drink with joy / the sober outpouring of the Spirit.*

**7**
> Laetus dies hic transeat:
> pudor sit ut diluculum:
> fides velut meridies:
> crepusculum mens nesciat.

*May this day pass in gladness: / our modesty like the dawn, / our faith like the noon, / and our mind know no twilight.*

**8**
> Aurora lucem provehit,
> cum luce nobis prodeat
> in Patre totus Filius,
> et totus in Verbo Pater.

*The dawn brings on the light; / with the light may he come forth to us: / the Son wholly in the Father, / and the Father wholly in the Word.*

**Doxology** (later addition)
> Deo Patri sit gloria,
> eiusque soli Filio,
> cum Spiritu Paraclito,
> nunc et per omne saeculum. Amen.

*Glory to God the Father, / and to his only Son, / with the Spirit, the Comforter, / now and through every age. Amen.*
