## The text

Latin as printed, with sentence capitals added for the screen. **Bold** = stressed syllable (ecclesiastical Latin).

### 1
> **Se**ro te a**ma**vi, pulchri**tu**do tam an**ti**qua et tam **no**va, **se**ro te a**ma**vi!

*Too late loved I Thee, O Thou Beauty of ancient days, yet ever new! too late I loved Thee!*

### 2
> Et **ec**ce **in**tus **e**ras et **e**go **fo**ris, et **i**bi te quae**re**bam, et in **is**ta for**mo**sa quae fe**cis**ti de**for**mis inru**e**bam.

*And behold, Thou wert within, and I abroad, and there I searched for Thee; deformed I, plunging amid those fair forms which Thou hadst made.*

### 3
> **Me**cum **e**ras, et **te**cum non **e**ram. **E**a me te**ne**bant **lon**ge a te, quae si in te non **es**sent, non **es**sent.

*Thou wert with me, but I was not with Thee. Things held me far from Thee, which, unless they were in Thee, were not at all.*

### 4
> Vo**cas**ti et cla**mas**ti et ru**pis**ti surdi**ta**tem **me**am; corus**cas**ti, splendu**is**ti et fu**gas**ti caeci**ta**tem **me**am;

*Thou calledst, and shoutedst, and burstest my deafness. Thou flashedst, shonest, and scatteredst my blindness.*

### 5
> fra**gras**ti, et **du**xi **spi**ritum et an**he**lo **ti**bi; gus**ta**vi et e**su**rio et **si**tio; teti**gis**ti me, et ex**ar**si in **pa**cem **tu**am.

*Thou breathedst odours, and I drew in breath and panted for Thee. I tasted, and hunger and thirst. Thou touchedst me, and I burned for Thy peace.*

**Lyrics (Latin):**
```
[Intro: low drone, muted trumpet]

[Verse 1: solo voice, almost unaccompanied]
Sero te amavi,
pulchritudo tam antiqua et tam nova,
sero te amavi!

[Verse 2]
Et ecce intus eras et ego foris,
et ibi te quaerebam,
et in ista formosa quae fecisti
deformis inruebam.

[Verse 3: bass enters]
Mecum eras, et tecum non eram.
Ea me tenebant longe a te,
quae si in te non essent, non essent.

[Instrumental: muted trumpet answers]

[Verse 4: building]
Vocasti et clamasti
et rupisti surditatem meam;
coruscasti, splenduisti
et fugasti caecitatem meam;

[Verse 5: fullest, then opening into light]
fragrasti, et duxi spiritum
et anhelo tibi;
gustavi et esurio et sitio;
tetigisti me,
et exarsi in pacem tuam.

[Outro: quiet, the opening returns]
Sero te amavi,
pulchritudo tam antiqua et tam nova,
sero te amavi.

[End: drone fades]
```
The outro repeats his own opening sentence unaltered (no added words).
