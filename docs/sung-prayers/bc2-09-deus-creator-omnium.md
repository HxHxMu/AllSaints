| Saint | St. Ambrose of Milan (c. 340–397), bishop, Doctor of the Church · feast December 7 |
| Attribution | **Written by** Ambrose. Walpole calls the evidence "irrefragable": St. Augustine names it as Ambrose's twice. ## The text

Latin as printed by Walpole, with capitals for the screen. English ours.

**1**
> Deus creator omnium
> polique rector, uestiens
> diem decoro lumine,
> noctem soporis gratia,

*God, Creator of all things / and Ruler of the heavens, who clothe / the day in lovely light, / the night in the gift of sleep,*

**2**
> artus solutos ut quies
> reddat laboris usui,
> mentesque fessas alleuet
> luctusque soluat anxios,

*so that rest may give our loosened limbs / back to their work, / lift up weary minds / and loosen anxious griefs:*

**3**
> grates peracto iam die
> et noctis exortu preces,
> uoti reos ut adiuues,
> hymnum canentes soluimus.

*our thanks, now the day is done, / and our prayers as night comes on, / that you may help us, bound by our vow, / we pay in singing this hymn.*

**4**
> Te cordis ima concinant,
> te uox canora concrepet,
> te diligat castus amor,
> te mens adoret sobria,

*Let the depths of the heart sing of you, / let the ringing voice sound out to you, / let chaste love love you, / let the sober mind adore you,*

**5**
> ut cum profunda clauserit
> diem caligo noctium,
> fides tenebras nesciat
> et nox fide reluceat.

*so that when the deep darkness of night / has closed in the day, / faith may know no darkness, / and night shine bright with faith.*

**6**
> Dormire mentem ne sinas,
> dormire culpa nouerit;
> castis fides refrigerans
> somni uaporem temperet.

*Do not let the soul fall asleep; / let sin be the one that sleeps. / May faith, cooling the pure, / temper the heat of sleep.*

**7**
> Exuta sensu lubrico
> te cordis ima somnient,
> nec hostis inuidi dolo
> pauor quietos suscitet.

*Free of wandering thoughts, / may the depths of the heart dream of you, / and let no fear, by the envious enemy's craft, / wake us from our rest.*

**8**
> Christum rogemus et Patrem,
> Christi Patrisque Spiritum,
> unum potens per omnia:
> foue precantes, Trinitas.

*Let us ask Christ and the Father, / and the Spirit of Christ and the Father, / One, mighty over all: / cherish those who pray, O Trinity.*

Notes for the description:
- St. Augustine quotes this hymn in the *Confessions* (IX.12): after his mother's death, it was these verses of Ambrose that eased his grief.
- St. Monica quoted its last line, *foue precantes, Trinitas*, in a conversation Augustine records in *De beata vita*.
- Stanza 3: the singers vowed in the morning to give thanks if brought safely through the day; at evening they "pay" the vow (Walpole's note).
- The hymn has no Amen in Walpole; none is added.

## Lyrics (for Suno or Gemini)

Sung spelling uses *v* (uestiens → vestiens, alleuet → allevet…); the screen keeps Walpole's *u*. Same words. About 3½–4 minutes at a slow pace. For Gemini, ask for "about 3 minutes" and check that stanza 8 made it in.

```
[Intro: low drone, single rhodes chord, like a lamp being lit]

[Verse 1: solo voice, almost unaccompanied]
Deus creator omnium
polique rector, vestiens
diem decoro lumine,
noctem soporis gratia,

[Verse 2]
artus solutos ut quies
reddat laboris usui,
mentesque fessas allevet
luctusque solvat anxios,

[Verse 3: bass enters]
grates peracto iam die
et noctis exortu preces,
voti reos ut adiuves,
hymnum canentes solvimus.

[Verse 4: warmer]
Te cordis ima concinant,
te vox canora concrepet,
te diligat castus amor,
te mens adoret sobria,

[Instrumental: muted trumpet answers]

[Verse 5: darker, then glowing]
ut cum profunda clauserit
diem caligo noctium,
fides tenebras nesciat
et nox fide reluceat.

[Verse 6: hushed]
Dormire mentem ne sinas,
dormire culpa noverit;
castis fides refrigerans
somni vaporem temperet.

[Verse 7: softest]
Exuta sensu lubrico
te cordis ima somnient,
nec hostis invidi dolo
pavor quietos suscitet.

[Verse 8: fullest, then fading]
Christum rogemus et Patrem,
Christi Patrisque Spiritum,
unum potens per omnia:
fove precantes, Trinitas.

[End: drone fades into night]
```
No repeats, no added words, no Amen.
