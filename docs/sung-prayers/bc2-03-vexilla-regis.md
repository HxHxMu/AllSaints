## The text

Latin as printed by Walpole, with capitals for the screen. English ours.

**1**
> Vexilla regis prodeunt,
> fulget crucis mysterium,
> quo carne carnis conditor
> suspensus est patibulo.

*The banners of the King go forth; / the mystery of the Cross shines out, / where the Maker of flesh, in the flesh, / was hung upon the gallows.*

**2**
> Confixa clauis uiscera
> tendens manus, uestigia,
> redemptionis gratia
> hic inmolata est hostia.

*His body pierced with nails, / his hands and feet stretched out, / for the sake of our redemption / here the Victim was offered.*

**3**
> Quo uulneratus insuper
> mucrone dirae lanceae,
> ut nos lauaret crimine,
> manauit unda, sanguine.

*Here, wounded further still / by the point of the cruel lance, / to wash us clean of sin / he poured out water and blood.*

**4**
> Inpleta sunt quae concinit
> Dauid fideli carmine,
> dicendo nationibus:
> "Regnauit a ligno Deus."

*Fulfilled is what David sang / in his faithful song, / telling the nations: / "God has reigned from the tree."*

**5**
> Arbor decora et fulgida,
> ornata regis purpura,
> electa digno stipite
> tam sancta membra tangere.

*O tree, beautiful and shining, / adorned with the purple of the King, / chosen, a worthy trunk, / to touch such holy limbs.*

**6**
> Beata, cuius bracchiis
> pretium pependit saeculi!
> Statera facta est corporis
> praedam tulitque tartari.

*Blessed tree, on whose arms / hung the ransom of the world! / It became the scale that weighed his body / and carried off the prey of hell.*

**7**
> Fundis aroma cortice,
> uincis sapore nectare,
> iocunda fructu fertili
> plaudis triumpho nobili.

*From your bark you pour out fragrance; / you are sweeter than nectar; / glad in your rich fruit, / you hail the noble triumph.*

**8**
> Salue ara, salue uictima
> de passionis gloria,
> qua uita mortem pertulit
> et morte uitam reddidit.

*Hail, altar! Hail, Victim, / for the glory of the Passion, / in which Life endured death / and by death gave back life.*

Notes for the description: "God has reigned from the tree" is Psalm 96(95):10 as the early Latin Church read it (*regnavit a ligno Deus*), quoted by Justin Martyr and Tertullian; the "purple" of stanza 5 is Christ's blood; "the scale" (*statera*) is Fortunatus's image of the Cross weighing out the world's ransom.

## Lyrics (for Suno or Gemini)

Sung spelling uses *v* (clavis, viscera…) and *impleta*; the screen keeps Walpole's *u* and *inpleta*. Same words.

```
[Intro: low drone, distant bell]

[Verse 1: solo voice, almost unaccompanied]
Vexilla regis prodeunt,
fulget crucis mysterium,
quo carne carnis conditor
suspensus est patibulo.

[Verse 2]
Confixa clavis viscera
tendens manus, vestigia,
redemptionis gratia
hic immolata est hostia.

[Verse 3: bass enters]
Quo vulneratus insuper
mucrone dirae lanceae,
ut nos lavaret crimine,
manavit unda, sanguine.

[Verse 4]
Impleta sunt quae concinit
David fideli carmine,
dicendo nationibus:
regnavit a ligno Deus.

[Instrumental: muted trumpet answers]

[Verse 5: low choir joins]
Arbor decora et fulgida,
ornata regis purpura,
electa digno stipite
tam sancta membra tangere.

[Verse 6]
Beata, cuius bracchiis
pretium pependit saeculi!
Statera facta est corporis
praedam tulitque tartari.

[Verse 7: warmer, rising]
Fundis aroma cortice,
vincis sapore nectare,
iocunda fructu fertili
plaudis triumpho nobili.

[Verse 8: fullest, then quiet]
Salve ara, salve victima
de passionis gloria,
qua vita mortem pertulit
et morte vitam reddidit.

[End: drone fades, one bell]
```
No repeats, no added words, no *O Crux ave* (not Fortunatus's). (The take sings a closing "Amen" anyway; kept, and credited as an addition.)
