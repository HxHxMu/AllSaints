## The text

Latin as printed in the 1922 Missal (*i* for *j*, as in the other sheets; the Missal's spelling *coena* kept). English ours.

**1a**
> Lauda, Sion, Salvatorem,
> lauda ducem et pastorem
> in hymnis et canticis.

*Praise, O Sion, your Saviour, / praise your leader and your shepherd / in hymns and canticles.*

**1b**
> Quantum potes, tantum aude:
> quia maior omni laude,
> nec laudare sufficis.

*Dare all you can: / he is greater than all praise, / and you can never praise him enough.*

**2a**
> Laudis thema specialis,
> panis vivus et vitalis
> hodie proponitur.

*A special theme of praise, / the living and life-giving Bread, / is set before us today:*

**2b**
> Quem in sacrae mensa coenae
> turbae fratrum duodenae
> datum non ambigitur.

*the Bread that, at the table of the holy Supper, / was given to the band of the twelve brothers. / Of this there is no doubt.*

**3a**
> Sit laus plena, sit sonora,
> sit iucunda, sit decora
> mentis iubilatio.
> Dies enim solemnis agitur,
> in qua mensae prima recolitur
> huius institutio.

*Let praise be full, let it ring out; / let the heart's rejoicing / be glad and fitting. / For this is the solemn day / that recalls the first founding / of this table.*

**3b**
> In hac mensa novi Regis,
> novum Pascha novae legis
> Phase vetus terminat.
> Vetustatem novitas,
> umbram fugat veritas,
> noctem lux eliminat.

*At this table of the new King, / the new Passover of the new law / brings the old Pasch to an end. / The new puts the old to flight, / the truth the shadow; / light drives out the night.*

**4a**
> Quod in coena Christus gessit,
> faciendum hoc expressit
> in sui memoriam.

*What Christ did at the Supper / he commanded to be done / in memory of him.*

**4b**
> Docti sacris institutis,
> panem, vinum in salutis
> consecramus hostiam.

*Taught by his holy commands, / we consecrate the bread and wine / as the Victim of salvation.*

**5a**
> Dogma datur Christianis,
> quod in carnem transit panis,
> et vinum in sanguinem.

*This teaching is given to Christians: / that bread passes into flesh, / and wine into blood.*

**5b**
> Quod non capis, quod non vides,
> animosa firmat fides,
> praeter rerum ordinem.

*What you cannot grasp, what you cannot see, / a living faith makes sure, / beyond the order of nature.*

**6a**
> Sub diversis speciebus,
> signis tantum, et non rebus,
> latent res eximiae.

*Under different appearances, / signs only and not the things themselves, / wondrous realities lie hidden.*

**6b**
> Caro cibus, sanguis potus:
> manet tamen Christus totus
> sub utraque specie.

*His flesh is food, his blood is drink, / yet Christ remains whole / under each kind.*

**7a**
> A sumente non concisus,
> non confractus, non divisus:
> integer accipitur.

*The one who receives him does not cut him, / break him or divide him: / he is received whole.*

**7b**
> Sumit unus, sumunt mille:
> quantum isti, tantum ille:
> nec sumptus consumitur.

*One receives, a thousand receive: / the one as much as all of them, / and received, he is not used up.*

**8a**
> Sumunt boni, sumunt mali:
> sorte tamen inaequali,
> vitae vel interitus.

*The good receive, the wicked receive, / but with unequal outcome: / life, or ruin.*

**8b**
> Mors est malis, vita bonis:
> vide paris sumptionis
> quam sit dispar exitus.

*Death to the wicked, life to the good: / see how unlike the end / of the same receiving.*

**9a**
> Fracto demum sacramento,
> ne vacilles, sed memento,
> tantum esse sub fragmento,
> quantum toto tegitur.

*When at last the Sacrament is broken, / do not waver, but remember: / there is as much in a fragment / as is hidden in the whole.*

**9b**
> Nulla rei fit scissura:
> signi tantum fit fractura:
> qua nec status nec statura
> signati minuitur.

*The reality is not divided: / only the sign is broken, / and he whom it signifies / is lessened neither in state nor in stature.*

**10a**
> Ecce panis Angelorum,
> factus cibus viatorum:
> vere panis filiorum,
> non mittendus canibus.

*Behold the Bread of Angels, / made the food of pilgrims: / truly the children's bread, / not to be thrown to the dogs.*

**10b**
> In figuris praesignatur,
> cum Isaac immolatur:
> agnus Paschae deputatur:
> datur manna patribus.

*It was foreshown in figures: / when Isaac is offered up, / when the Passover lamb is set apart, / when manna is given to our fathers.*

**11a**
> Bone pastor, panis vere,
> Iesu, nostri miserere:
> tu nos pasce, nos tuere:
> tu nos bona fac videre
> in terra viventium.

*Good Shepherd, true Bread, / Jesus, have mercy on us: / feed us, guard us, / let us see good things / in the land of the living.*

**11b**
> Tu, qui cuncta scis et vales,
> qui nos pascis hic mortales:
> tuos ibi commensales,
> coheredes et sodales
> fac sanctorum civium.
> Amen. Alleluia.

*You who know all things and can do all things, / who feed us here, mortal as we are: / make us, your own, your guests at table there, / co-heirs and companions / of the holy citizens of heaven. / Amen. Alleluia.*

Notes for the description:
- A sequence is a hymn sung at Mass before the Gospel. Only four (five with the *Stabat Mater*) were kept in the Roman Missal after the 16th century, and *Lauda Sion* is one of them.
- 3b: *Phase* is the old word for the Passover (Hebrew *Pesach*).
- 10a: "the children's bread, not to be thrown to the dogs", from Matthew 15:26.
- 10b: three Old Testament figures of the Eucharist: Isaac (Genesis 22), the Passover lamb (Exodus 12), the manna (Exodus 16).
- 11a: "the land of the living", from Psalm 27(26):13.

## On screen

24 screens, one strophe each, Latin above, our English below; or 11 screens, one pair each, if the pairs fit (3a/3b and 11a/11b are the longest). One strophe per screen reads better.

## Style

```
contemplative gregorian jazz, solo male chant vocal in latin, festal and radiant, a procession of the blessed sacrament, plainchant phrasing, gentle trochaic lilt, major mode with lydian light, warm drone, soft rhodes, upright bass, flugelhorn answering the voice, distant bells, cathedral reverb, joyful, confident, adoring, slow and steady, no drum groove, no pop chorus
```

## Lyrics (for Suno)

Each pair of strophes is one section, as in the chant, where *a* and *b* share the same music. Too long for Gemini (~3 min).

**How to make it (recommended):** generate **Part 1** (strophes 1a–6b, about 4½ min) in Suno, pick the best take, then use **Extend** from its end with the **Part 2** lyrics (7a–11b). Extend keeps the same voice and melody, so the two halves sound like one track. Then use "Get whole song".

**If you'd rather keep it short:** sing only 1, 2, 10 and 11 (about 3 min), and say "selected strophes" on screen and in the description. I'd only do this if Extend doesn't work.
