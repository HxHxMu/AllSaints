# Lives of the Saints · Series 2 · St. John of God (1495–1550)

Feast Mar 8 (also his birthday and death day). Release idea: Parts I–VI Tue Mar 2 – Sun Mar 7, 2027 (Lent), 7:30 PM ET; full 16:9 film Mon Mar 8.
Sources: Francisco de Castro's Life (1585, chaplain of his hospital); Catholic Encyclopedia; Wikipedia. Legend labelled as tradition.

I · The Runaway — Portugal, 1495. At eight he vanishes from home, following a travelling cleric into Spain; he is left with a shepherd in Oropesa. His first biographer says his mother died of grief soon after. Grown, his master offers him his daughter; he runs off to be a soldier.
   Next: "the gallows."
II · The Gallows — Under the Count of Oropesa against the French at Fuenterrabía. Set to guard the spoils, they are stolen; he is condemned to hang, and an officer spares him. Shepherd again, then soldier against the Turks. He walks home to Portugal: his parents are gone.
   Next: "the crossing."
III · The Crossing — In Ceuta he serves an exiled, ruined Portuguese family, labouring on the walls to feed them. Back in Gibraltar he peddles holy books and pictures from town to town. Tradition holds a child met him on the road, called him John of God and sent him to Granada. He opens a bookshop there.
   Next: "the madman."
IV · The Madman — 20 January 1537, feast of St. Sebastian: John of Ávila preaches. John cries out for mercy, gives away his books, runs through the streets. Granada takes him for mad: the Royal Hospital, chains, the whip. He sees how the sick are treated. John of Ávila visits him and turns his penance toward the poor.
   Next: "the house."
V · The House — Pilgrimage to Our Lady of Guadalupe. Back in Granada he rents a house for the sick, carries them in on his shoulders, begs for them by night. He keeps giving his cloak away; the Bishop of Tuy gives him a habit, and the name for good: John of God.
   Next: "the river."
VI · The River — Fire in the Royal Hospital: he carries the sick out through the flames. In flood, a young man falls into the Genil; John goes in after him, and cannot save him. Fever follows. He dies 8 March 1550, his 55th birthday. Canonized 1690; patron of hospitals, the sick and the dying. "Saint John of God, pray for us."
